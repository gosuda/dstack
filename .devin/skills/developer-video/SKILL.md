# Developer video

Prepare everything a human needs to record a technical video: script or storyboard, chapter plan, demo runbook, accessibility checklist, and title brief. You prepare in chat only. Nothing gets recorded, uploaded, promoted, or managed here, and the plan never depends on a paid vendor. This stays separate from text-post skills because video artifacts and their constraints genuinely differ.

## What you need before you start

- `topic` (required): the technical subject with supplied material, or an explicitly requested read-only research pass.
- `format` (optional): tutorial, demo, talk, or explainer. Defaults to demo when omitted.
- `length` (optional): target duration band. Defaults to the shortest band that fits the runbook.

## From topic to pack

1. Fix the format, the audience, and the one viewer outcome. All three stay singular and explicit. When the topic is too large for the band, split it into episodes with a per-episode outcome.
2. Write the script or storyboard beat by beat, naming what the viewer sees and hears in each beat.
3. Lay out chapters with timecodes proportional to the length band so they cover the full runtime with no gap.
4. Write the demo runbook: exact commands, inputs, expected outputs, and a recovery line for each likeliest live failure. Mark every demo claim source-backed or explicitly unverified. A demo that cannot be verified is marked with what verification needs, never scripted as certain.
5. Complete the accessibility checklist: captions, transcript plan, contrast and font size for on-screen text, and audio clarity notes. Each item passes or is flagged for production.
6. Draft the title and thumbnail brief from the viewer outcome, with no clickbait the video does not deliver.

## What you hand over

A video pack with the script or storyboard, chapters and timecodes, the demo runbook with expected outputs, the accessibility checklist, the title and thumbnail brief, and the source-backing record for every demo claim.
