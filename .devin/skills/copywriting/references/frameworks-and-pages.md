# Frameworks and page types

Read this in page mode before writing or auditing marketing copy. Product-state strings and action labels use `product-state-strings.md` instead.

## The nine frameworks

Pick the lead framework with the routing table in the main skill file, then layer others where they help.

| Framework | Rule |
|---|---|
| Why/How/What (Simon Sinek) | Lead with why the reader should care, then how, then what the product is |
| Problem, agitate, solution (PAS) | Name the pain, amplify its cost, present the solution |
| AIDA | Attention, Interest, Desire, Action, for cold traffic |
| StoryBrand | The customer is the hero and the product is the guide. Never make the product the hero |
| Before, after, bridge (BAB) | Show the before state, the after state, and the product as the bridge. Warmer and more aspirational than PAS |
| Show don't tell | Replace each adjective with a specific fact, number, or scenario |
| Benefit not feature | Lead with the outcome for the user; mention the mechanism only once the benefit is clear |
| Sentence economy | Every sentence earns its space. Cut openers like "In order to" and "It is important to note that" |
| CTA clarity | Action verb, plus what the reader gets, plus a qualifier. Never two CTAs with the same verb on one screen |

## Page-type norms

Apply these when the page type is known.

### Homepage

Goal: establish what the product is and who it is for. Pick the highest-value segment and write for them. Sections, in order: hero (lead with Why), social proof above the fold, problem or pain, solution and benefits (one benefit per point), how it works, testimonials, final CTA. Do not add a secondary CTA that dilutes the primary action.

### Landing page

Goal: drive one action, with a message that matches what brought the reader. The headline mirrors the ad or email promise. Use PAS for problem-aware traffic and AIDA for cold traffic. One CTA only; strip navigation and footer links.

### Pricing page

Goal: help visitors choose. Name plans by buyer type, not tier ("Solo / Team / Company" beats "Basic / Pro / Enterprise"). Sections: value restatement, plan comparison (2 to 4 plans), feature differentiators, FAQ, social proof by tier, and risk reversal near the CTA.

### Feature page

Goal: connect a feature to an outcome for visitors who are already evaluating. Follow the chain from feature to benefit to outcome. Skip broad setup and go straight to the specific outcome, with a number or an example.

### About page

Goal: build trust. Every element passes the "so what does this mean for me?" test. Include the mission stated as a customer benefit, an origin story tied to the customer's frustration, a human team, 3 to 5 values that matter to customers, and a CTA that points to the product.
