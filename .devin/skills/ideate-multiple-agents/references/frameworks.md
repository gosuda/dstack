# Ideation lenses

No idea needs every lens. Pick the one that fits what is stuck, and leave the rest alone.

- SCAMPER: reshape something that already exists by substitute, combine, adapt, modify (magnify or minimize), put to other uses, eliminate, and reverse or rearrange. Best for improving existing products. Weak for greenfield ideas.
- How Might We: reframe a pain point as "How might we" naming a specific user, a desired outcome, and the constraint being lifted. A good frame is narrow enough to act on and broad enough to admit surprise, and it carries a tension that forces creativity. Best for breaking fixation on one solution.
- First Principles Thinking: list what is known true, list every assumption, throw out each assumption that is convention rather than law, and rebuild from what remains. Best for breaking incremental-only thinking.
- Jobs To Be Done: state the functional, emotional, and social job as When plus I want plus so I can. People hire products to do a job; the real competitor is the current workaround. Best when the right problem is not yet in view.
- Constraint-Based Ideation: impose time, feature, tech, cost, audience, or scale limits to force invention. Best for cutting an idea that has grown too large or vague.
- Pre-mortem: assume the project shipped and failed, list every plausible technical, market, team, and timing cause, decide which are preventable and which would kill it, and name what is accepted. Best for pressure-testing an idea that feels good but unexamined.
- Analogous Inspiration: borrow structure, not surface, from other industries, products, natural systems, or history. Best for Phase 1 expansion with variations that feel genuinely different.
